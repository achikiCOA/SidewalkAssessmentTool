Before You Start

  1. Location: choose Point Issue for one spot or Sidewalk Segment for a longer stretch, then confirm the map location.
  2. Photos: take one or more photos. Add a close-up and a context photo when possible.
  3. Condition: choose a preset or issue type and severity.
  4. Context: add pedestrian volume, school/transit proximity, and comments.
  5. Measurements: add measurements if available; otherwise skip to contact.
  6. Contact: confirm your name or email, review the report, then submit.
Field Definitions & References

Point Issue

  • Use for one specific spot.
  • Examples: single trip hazard, blocked sidewalk, broken slab, curb ramp issue, or missing detectable warning.

Sidewalk Segment

  • Use for a longer continuous stretch with the same general problem.
  • Examples: repeated heaving, cracking, narrow passage, or several damaged slabs in a row.

Severity

  • Fair: usable, minor defect or early wear.
  • Poor: clear defect affecting comfort, drainage, or accessibility.
  • Severe: major barrier, trip risk, blocked path, or likely ADA concern.
  • Missing: sidewalk, curb ramp, or accessible connection is absent.

Pedestrian Volume

  • Low: occasional pedestrian use.
  • Medium: regular use during part of the day.
  • High: frequent use, downtown area, school route, transit stop, park, or civic destination.

School/Transit Proximity

  • Yes: near a school, bus stop, transit stop, marked school route, or key public destination.
  • No: no known school or transit connection nearby.
  • Unknown: use when proximity is not clear in the field.

Detectable Warning

  • Present: truncated dome surface appears intact and visible.
  • Missing: curb ramp lacks a detectable warning surface where one is expected.
  • Damaged/Worn or Low Contrast: warning surface may not be reliably detectable.

ADA/PROWAG quick references: PROWAG technical requirements, ADA curb ramp guide, 2010 ADA Standards.

Common field checks include 48 in. minimum pedestrian access route width in PROWAG, sidewalk running slope less than 5%, maximum cross slope of 2%, curb ramp running slope up to 8.3%, and surface openings or vertical changes that may affect accessibility. Always verify current federal, state, and local standards before final design or enforcement.

Privacy Notice

This tool can collect GPS coordinates, walking paths, photos, optional notes, and optional contact information. Data is saved in this browser first. If an upload endpoint is configured, submitted point inspections may be sent to that endpoint. Recorder GeoJSON exports do not include inspector names unless you turn that option on before exporting.
